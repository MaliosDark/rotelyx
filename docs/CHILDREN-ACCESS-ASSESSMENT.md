# Children's Access Assessment

**Service:** Rotelyx
**Provider:** Ideoa Labs
**Assessed:** 3 September 2026
**Next review:** 3 September 2027, or on any substantial product change

Prepared against the Online Safety Act 2023 duties for user-to-user services
and the ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code. **This is not legal advice and was
not written by a lawyer.** It is the provider's own record of what the service
does, what a child using it would be exposed to, and what the architecture does
about it. Every claim about behaviour names the code that implements it, so a
reviewer can check rather than take it on trust.

---

## 1. What the service is

A messenger. Two people who already know each other exchange text, pictures,
files and voice calls. Everything is end to end encrypted.

**Regulated service type:** user-to-user, under section 3 of the Act. Users
encounter content generated by other users.

**Not in scope of this document:** search services, pornography, paid content.
Rotelyx has none.

## 2. Can children access it?

**Yes.** There is no age gate and no barrier that would stop a child using it.

We do not claim otherwise. The service is distributed through application
stores with an age rating, and a store rating is not access control.

**Are significant numbers of children likely to use it?** We have no way to
know and will not pretend to. Rotelyx collects no age, no identity, no
demographic of any kind, and cannot count its users by any attribute. Rather
than guess, this assessment proceeds as though children do use it, which is
the position Ofcom's guidance arrives at for services without highly effective
age assurance.

## 3. Age assurance

**None, deliberately.**

Rotelyx has no accounts, no phone numbers, no email addresses and no identity
registry. Determining a user's age would require introducing at least one of
those, or a third party verification provider, either of which would create
exactly the identity record the service exists not to hold.

We do not use a self-declared age box. Ofcom is explicit that self-declaration
is not highly effective age assurance, and a box that verifies nothing while
appearing to is worse than none: it produces a record, it invites a lie, and it
lets the provider claim a protection it does not have.

**The consequence is accepted rather than argued around.** Without highly
effective age assurance the service is treated as likely to be accessed by
children, and the protections below are applied to every user without
distinction. The ICO permits this approach expressly: a service that does not
wish to determine age individually may instead apply the standards appropriate
to children to all of its users.

## 4. What a child is exposed to, and what they are not

### Contact

**Nobody can reach a user who has not first invited them.** This is not a
setting. There is no directory to be listed in, no username to be searched, no
phone number to be matched, and no suggestion of people a user might know. The
mailbox that carries messages derives its delivery tags from an existing
conversation, so it has no way to deliver anything from a stranger:
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "the mailbox delivers only to people who already share
a conversation".

The consequences for a child are direct. There is no mechanism by which an
adult who does not already have an invitation can initiate contact. There is no
"people nearby", no public profile to be found through, no channel or community
to be approached in.

**Residual risk:** an invitation is a piece of text or a QR code, and a child
can pass one to anybody, including through another service. Rotelyx cannot see
that happen and cannot prevent it. This is the principal residual risk in this
assessment and it is not mitigated by the architecture.

### Content

There is no feed, no recommendation, no trending, no discovery surface and no
algorithmic ranking of any kind. A user sees what people they have invited send
them, in the order it was sent, and nothing else. There is no route by which
content from an unknown party reaches a user's screen.

**Residual risk:** a contact a child has already accepted can send them
anything, including content that is harmful or illegal. Rotelyx cannot see it,
cannot classify it and cannot intervene in it. See section 6.

### Commercial exploitation

No advertising. No behavioural profiling. No tracking. No analytics. No data
broker relationship. No in-app purchases directed at users. This is verifiable
rather than promised: `test/no_foreign_infrastructure_test.dart` fails the
build if the client gains the ability to contact any host other than the
mailbox, and every permitted host in it carries a written reason.

### Data

Message content is end to end encrypted with MLS (RFC 9420) and cannot be read
by Rotelyx, by any server operator, or by anybody who obtains the servers.
History is stored on the user's own device, sealed, and only when the user
chooses to keep it.

No IP address is disclosed to another user. Calls refuse to start on a direct
network path precisely because a direct path would hand the other person the
device's address; the refusal is in the media layer rather than in a caller
that could forget it, and there is no configuration that changes it.

## 5. Controls a user has, and what each one does

Each of these is in the shipped client and was verified for this assessment.

| Control | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Delete this conversation | Removes every message and the cryptographic session | `lib/ui/screens/contact.dart` |
| Remove a member | An MLS commit: the removed member cannot decrypt anything sent afterwards | `lib/rotelyx/rotelyx_service.dart`, `removeMember` |
| Delete for everyone | Withdraws a sent message from the other side | `lib/ui/screens/chat.dart` |
| Disappearing messages | Messages expire on a timer chosen per conversation | `lib/rotelyx/burn_clock.dart` |
| Application lock | A PIN, separate from the device unlock | `lib/rotelyx/rotelyx_store.dart` |
| Ghost mode | Stops writing anything to the device until the application is closed | `lib/ui/screens/settings.dart` |
| Safety number | Confirms the other side is who they were when the conversation began, and changes visibly if the key changes | `lib/ui/screens/chat.dart` |

**On blocking.** Rotelyx has no block list because it does not need one.
Deleting a conversation removes the session, and with no account, no directory
and a mailbox that delivers only within an existing conversation, there is
nothing left for the other person to reach. This is a stronger outcome than a
block list in a service with accounts, where the blocked account continues to
exist and can be used to try again.

## 6. What the service cannot do, stated plainly

**Rotelyx cannot read, scan, moderate or classify the content of any
conversation.** It does not hold the keys. This is the design and it is not
adjustable by us, by a court order, or by anybody who takes the servers.

The consequences are accepted rather than minimised:

- Content-based moderation is impossible. No filter, no hash matching, no
  classifier, no human review of message contents.
- A report of message content cannot be acted on by us, because we cannot see
  the content being reported and have no account to attach it to.
- We cannot suspend a user, because there is no account to suspend.

**What we can do:** act on reports about the service itself, its behaviour, its
distribution or its infrastructure. That route is the contact page and it is
answered by a person.

We do not offer an in-application report button for message content. A button
that appears to send a report somewhere, when nothing can be sent and nothing
can be acted on, is a safety feature in appearance only, and a child who
pressed it would be worse off for believing something had happened.

## 7. Risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | What reduces it | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contact by an unknown adult | Low | No directory, no search, no discovery, no public presence. Contact is impossible without an invitation | A child can pass an invitation to anybody, through any other service |
| Grooming by an accepted contact | Present | Removal is immediate and cryptographic; disappearing messages; safety numbers surface a changed key | Content is invisible to us. Nothing here detects the behaviour |
| Harmful or illegal content in a private conversation | Present | No route from a stranger; contact removable | We cannot see it. No filter or review is possible |
| Commercial exploitation, profiling, advertising | None | None exist in the product; a test fails the build if third party contact is added | None |
| Excessive data collected about a child | None | No identity, no age, no contacts uploaded, no analytics | None |
| A compromised or shared device | Present | Application lock, ghost mode, disappearing messages | An unlocked device shows what is on it, as with any application |
| Design that nudges a child toward less privacy | None | The private settings are the defaults, and the properties that matter are not settings at all | None |

## 8. Conclusion

Rotelyx is likely to be accessed by children and is treated accordingly. It
implements no age assurance and does not claim to. Instead, the protections
appropriate to a child user are applied to every user, which the ICO permits
and which this architecture arrives at without a child-specific mode: high
privacy by default, no data collected that could be misused, no commercial
interest in attention, and no route by which a stranger can reach anybody.

The material residual risk is what a child does with an invitation, and what an
already-known contact sends them. Neither is visible to us and we do not claim
otherwise.
