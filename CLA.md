# Contributor Licence Agreement

Rotelyx is AGPL-3.0-only, and it is also published in application stores whose
terms that licence cannot satisfy on its own. Publishing it in both places
means the project has to be able to grant itself an exception, and it can only
do that over code it holds the rights to.

That is the whole reason this document exists. It is not about owning your
work.

## What you keep

**Your copyright.** You wrote it, it stays yours, and your name stays on it in
the history. You may use your contribution anywhere else, for anything, under
any terms you like. Nothing here takes that away or asks you to stop.

## What you grant

By contributing, you grant Ideoa Labs a perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable,
royalty-free licence to use, reproduce, modify, distribute and sublicense your
contribution, **including the right to release it under a different licence**.

The last part is the operative one. Without it a single contribution makes the
project permanently unable to ship in a store, because the exception could no
longer be granted over the whole of it. With it, the code you send can go
everywhere the rest of the project goes.

## What you confirm

- The contribution is your own work, or you have the right to submit it.
- It is not covered by an agreement with an employer or anybody else that
  would conflict with the grant above.
- To your knowledge it does not infringe anybody's rights.
- You are not asking for payment, and none is offered.

## What this does not do

- It does not make your contribution proprietary. Rotelyx is AGPL-3.0-only and
  every line of it, including yours, stays published under that licence.
- It does not let anybody take the project closed. The AGPL release already
  made is irrevocable: what is out stays out, and anybody may fork it.
- It does not oblige you to maintain anything.

## How to accept

Comment on your pull request with:

    I have read CLA.md and I accept it.

Once, for all of your contributions. If your employer owns your work, have
somebody who can bind them say so on the thread instead.

## If you would rather not

Say so. Open an issue describing the change instead of a pull request, and it
will be written separately. An idea is not a contribution in the copyright
sense, and there is nothing to sign for one.
