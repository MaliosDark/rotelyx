Meet a phone

A phone has no address to connect to. It moves between networks and is asleep most of the time, so it is reached through a mailbox instead, and this is the way to talk to one. Messages only: a mailbox holds envelopes and hands them over when somebody asks, which carries a message that arrives in a second and cannot carry a call.

The phone can show one instead. Spaces and capitals do not matter.

Where the two of you meet. It sees sealed envelopes addressed to opaque tags and never a message.

Tell them when their messages were read

Off by default, and the phone client is too. A receipt is one more envelope every time something is read, and envelopes are what the operator of a mailbox can count. With this off, their messages show as delivered and never as read, which is the honest picture rather than a tick nobody sent.

Who is here

Everybody the conversation currently holds. Removing somebody is a commit: everyone moves to a key schedule derived without that leaf, and everyone sees it happen. It does not reach backwards, so they keep what they could already read.

Relay, and withdrawing an invitation

Leave empty for a direct connection. A call needs a relay: over a direct path the other side sees this machine's address.

Blocking somebody is withdrawing the invitation they came in on. There is no identity to ban: a caller arrives on a key belonging to one invitation and nothing else. Withdrawing stops the next connection, not one already open.

Trust boundary

This window and the cryptography run in the same process. Plaintext never crosses a socket, which is the difference from the browser harness.

What still sees your messages:

  • This device, and anything with code execution on it
  • The operating system
  • Whoever you are talking to

Rotelyx is unaudited. It makes no security claim until an independent cryptographic review is complete.
